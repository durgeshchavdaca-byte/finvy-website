Can a US Citizen Buy or Own Property in India? (2026): OCI Route, Foreign Nationals Living in India, Inheritance, Tax in India and What to Report in the US, with an OCI card, yes; without one, usually not.

A US citizen with an OCI card (or otherwise of Indian origin) can buy residential and commercial property in India, like an NRI, but not agricultural land, farmhouses or plantations. A US citizen who is not of Indian origin can buy only if resident in India under FEMA (for example, living and working in India), and otherwise only through inheritance or with RBI permission. Rent and gains from Indian property are taxed in India, and the US also taxes them as worldwide income with a foreign tax credit; Indian accounts and assets may need FBAR and Form 8938 reporting.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a US citizen buy property in India?
Yes if they hold an OCI card or are of Indian origin (not agricultural land), or if they are resident in India under FEMA. A US citizen of non-Indian origin living abroad cannot buy without RBI permission but can inherit.
Can an OCI cardholder buy agricultural land?
No. OCIs can buy residential and commercial property but not agricultural land, farmhouses or plantation property; they can inherit them.
Does a US citizen pay tax in India on Indian rent?
Yes. Rent from Indian property is taxable in India, and the tenant deducts TDS for non-residents. The US also taxes it, with a foreign tax credit.
Do I report Indian property to the IRS?
Property itself is not reported on FBAR, but Indian bank accounts holding rent or sale proceeds are, if foreign accounts exceed USD 10,000 in aggregate; income goes on the US return, and Form 8938 may apply.
