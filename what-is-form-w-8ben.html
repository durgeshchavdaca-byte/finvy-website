What is Form W-8BEN? Withholding certificate for non-US persons

Plain-English explanation of Form W-8BEN, who must file it, what treaty benefits it claims, and how it interacts with US withholding tax.

Overview

What is Form W-8BEN? Withholding certificate for non-US persons

Form W-8BEN (Certificate of Foreign Status of Beneficial Owner) is the IRS form non-US individuals submit to a US withholding agent to certify their non-US status and to claim a reduced rate of withholding under a tax treaty. Form W-8BEN-E is the entity-level equivalent. The form is not filed with the IRS - it is provided to the US payer (bank, broker, employer, customer), who keeps it on file and applies the correct withholding rate. Without a valid W-8BEN, US payers withhold at the default 30% rate on US-source income to non-US persons.

When you need to give a W-8BEN

Situations that trigger the form

Indian individuals provide W-8BEN to: US brokerages holding US stocks (e.g., Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab) to reduce dividend withholding from 30% to 25% under the India-US treaty; US payers of royalties, interest or fees for technical services to claim treaty-cap rates; US employers paying compensation to Indian contractors. Form W-8BEN-E is provided by Indian entities (companies, LLPs, trusts) to claim treaty benefits at the entity level, particularly important for software vendors and consultants invoicing US customers.

What the form actually claims

Foreign status plus treaty-rate election

Part I confirms identity, country of citizenship and tax residence, foreign tax identification (PAN for Indians). Part II claims treaty benefits: country of residence, treaty article (e.g., Article 12 for royalties under the India-US treaty), and the withholding rate claimed. The form requires the filer to certify that they meet the limitation-of-benefits article of the treaty (where applicable). Sign and date. The form is typically valid for three calendar years after signing; the US payer asks for refresh annually or when circumstances change.

Default withholding without W-8BEN

Why you do not skip this

Without a valid W-8BEN on file, the US payer must withhold at the statutory rates: 30% on dividends, interest, royalties and fees for technical services paid to non-US persons. Many Indian founders selling SaaS to US customers, freelance consultants invoicing US firms, and dividend-receiving stockholders unknowingly lose 30% of gross payments because they did not file W-8BEN with the payer. Recover-via-tax-return is possible but slow and uncertain; provide W-8BEN upfront.

W-8BEN-E for entities

Layered complexity for FATCA

W-8BEN-E is multi-page (8 pages of certifications) because it also serves as the FATCA classification document. Indian companies must classify themselves under FATCA: Active NFFE (non-financial foreign entity with active trade/business income), Passive NFFE, Direct Reporting NFFE, or various FFI sub-types. Most Indian operating businesses are Active NFFEs. The entity also claims treaty benefits in the same form. Errors in FATCA classification trigger US-side reporting issues; have a CA review the W-8BEN-E before sending to a major US customer.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Who needs to file W-8BEN?
Non-US individuals receiving US-source income (dividends, interest, royalties, fees) from a US payer. Indian individuals selling to US customers, holding US securities, or receiving US dividends provide W-8BEN to the US payer to access treaty rates.

Where do I file Form W-8BEN?
You don't file it with the IRS. You provide it to the US payer (bank, broker, customer, employer). The payer keeps it on file and applies the correct withholding rate. Each US payer needs their own copy.

How long is Form W-8BEN valid?
Generally three calendar years from the date of signing, plus the remainder of the year signed. So a form signed in March 2024 is valid through 31 December 2027. Refresh annually if the US payer requires, or earlier if circumstances change (residency, treaty position).

What is the difference between W-8BEN and W-8BEN-E?
W-8BEN is for non-US individuals (one page). W-8BEN-E is for non-US entities including Indian companies (eight pages) and includes FATCA classification along with treaty claims. Get the right form for the recipient type.

Can a foreign company use W-8BEN-E to claim full exemption from US withholding?
Only to the extent the treaty allows. For business profits earned without a US permanent establishment, Article 7 of the India-US treaty allows full exemption claimed on W-8BEN-E. For royalties and FTS, treaty caps reduce but do not eliminate withholding.

What happens if my W-8BEN expires?
The US payer will revert to 30% backup withholding until a refreshed W-8BEN is provided. Recover the over-withheld amount through US tax return filing (Form 1040-NR for individuals, 1120-F for foreign corporations) - slow and uncertain. Refresh proactively before expiry.
