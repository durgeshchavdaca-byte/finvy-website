Can a US CPA Firm Outsource Tax Returns to India? Section 7216 Consent Checklist, yes, with consent, disclosure and security in place.

Yes. A US CPA firm or enrolled agent can use preparers in India, but the taxpayer must first sign a separate Section 7216 consent to disclose return information to a preparer outside the United States, worded as Rev. Proc. 2013-14 requires for 1040-series returns. The firm must also tell clients it uses a third-party service provider, keep a confidentiality agreement with that provider, and cover it in its written information security plan.

/ The checklist

Before the first return goes offshore.

  • IRC Section 7216 consent: before tax return information goes to a preparer outside the United States, the taxpayer must sign a separate, dated consent. For Form 1040-series returns, Rev. Proc. 2013-14 prescribes the wording.
  • Social Security numbers: a full SSN can go offshore only if the consent specifically allows it; otherwise it is redacted before files leave your firm.
  • Duration: a consent that names no period lasts one year; give the client a copy when it is signed.
  • AICPA: the Code's interpretation on using a third-party service provider requires you to tell clients, before sharing confidential information, that a service provider may be used (an engagement-letter clause works), and to have a confidentiality agreement with the provider.
  • FTC Safeguards Rule: tax and accounting firms need a written information security plan (IRS Publication 5708 is a template) that covers service providers. A breach of unencrypted data affecting 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days.
  • Signing: your firm reviews and signs the return and files it from your own e-file account; we prepare under your review.

/ Practical set-up

What firms usually do.

  • Add a third-party service provider clause to engagement letters.
  • Collect the Section 7216 offshore consent with the organizer, separately signed and dated, and keep the copies.
  • Redact SSNs unless the consent covers them, or have the offshore team work only inside your systems.
  • Update the WISP to name the provider, access controls, multi-factor authentication and incident reporting.
  • Sign a confidentiality agreement and data-security terms with the provider.

Sources: IRS Rev. Proc. 2013-14; CPA Journal: outsourcing tax preparation overseas; Journal of Accountancy: third-party service providers; IRS Publication 5708 (WISP template).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is it legal for a CPA firm to outsource tax returns to India?
Yes, provided the taxpayer gives Section 7216 consent before return information is disclosed to a preparer outside the US, and the firm meets AICPA confidentiality and FTC Safeguards Rule requirements.
Does the client have to consent to offshore tax preparation?
Yes. A separate, signed and dated Section 7216 consent is required before disclosure to a preparer located outside the United States.
Can SSNs be sent to an offshore preparer?
Only if the consent specifically authorises it; otherwise the SSN must be redacted.
How long does a Section 7216 consent last?
For the period it states; if it states none, one year from signing.
