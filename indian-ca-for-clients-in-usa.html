/ NRI taxation for residents of USA

Indian tax when you live abroad.

  • US citizens and green-card holders are taxed by the US on worldwide income and by India on Indian-source income; double tax is relieved through foreign tax credits under the India-US DTAA.
  • Indian mutual funds held by US persons are usually PFICs; Indian bank and demat accounts count toward FBAR and FATCA reporting.
  • Selling Indian property: TDS under Section 195, lower deduction certificate under Section 197, then Form 15CA/15CB (Forms 145/146 from 1 April 2026) to move up to USD 1 million a year out of NRO.
  • Treaty withholding caps: dividends 15%/25%, interest 10%/15%, royalties and fees for included services 10%/15%.

/ What changed in 2026

Rules to know before you engage.

  • The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, with new section numbers: TDS on payments to non-residents is now section 393(2) (was 195), and the capital gains exemption on a house is section 82 (was 54). Section mapping.
  • New form numbers: Form 15CA/15CB are now Forms 145/146, Form 10F is Form 41 and the lower TDS certificate (Form 13) is Form 128. Form lookup.
  • Selling property in India: from 1 October 2026 the buyer deducts TDS using PAN in Form 141, with no TAN needed. What changed.
  • Foreign investment: Press Note 2 (2026) lets investors with non-controlling land-border beneficial ownership within 10% use the automatic route. Explainer.
  • Paying Indian freelancers or service providers: exporters must now receive payment within nine months of the invoice. FEMA export rules.
  • Returning NRIs with assets abroad: FAST-DS 2026 lets you declare unreported foreign assets by 31 December 2026. FAST-DS guide.
  • US residents sending money to India: the 1% US remittance excise tax applies only to cash-funded transfers, not bank or card transfers. Details.
  • US CPA firms and enrolled agents: we work as your offshore preparation and bookkeeping team, with Section 7216 consent in place. Details.
