OCI cardholder tax, what residence status really means.

OCI (Overseas Citizen of India) card gives Indian-origin persons lifetime multi-entry visa + near-citizen rights. But OCI status does NOT change your Indian tax residence — tax residence is determined only by Section 6 physical presence in India. OCI holder can be NRI (most common), RNOR, or Resident for a given FY. Here's the complete 2026 tax framework for OCI cardholders.

/ OCI card vs tax residence

They are separate.

OCI card is an immigration/visa document:

  • Issued by Government of India, Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Lifetime multi-entry Indian visa (no need for Indian visa before each trip).
  • Near-equal civil rights with Indian citizens (except voting, government jobs, agricultural land purchase, political office).
  • Does NOT confer Indian citizenship.
  • Does NOT determine Indian tax residence.

Indian tax residence is determined by Section 6 Income Tax Act:

  • Resident: physically in India for 182+ days in FY, OR 60+ days in FY plus 365+ days in preceding 4 FYs.
  • Non-Resident (NRI): neither test satisfied.
  • RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident): resident this FY but non-resident for 9+ of 10 preceding FYs, OR physically in India <= 729 days in 7 preceding FYs. See our RNOR guide.

So an OCI holder physically present in India for most of the FY = Resident; one who visits briefly = NRI. OCI card itself is tax-neutral.

/ Taxability depends on status

Three different regimes.

  • OCI = Resident: global income taxable in India. World-wide investment income, overseas rental, foreign employment salary, foreign capital gain — all taxable at slab rate (foreign tax credit under Section 90/91 for DTAA).
  • OCI = RNOR: India-source + foreign-source income from business controlled from India + professional set up in India. Foreign-source investment / overseas employment / foreign capital gain remain exempt. See RNOR guide. RNOR status typically available for 2-3 years post-return to India.
  • OCI = NRI: only India-source income taxable — Indian employment, Indian business, Indian property rental, India-source capital gain, Indian bank interest. Foreign income / foreign investment income not taxable in India.

Many OCI holders maintain NRI status throughout life (short Indian visits only) → only India-source income taxable. Significant tax planning benefit vs full-resident status.

/ Key tax touch-points for OCI holder

What they typically own in India.

  • Indian bank account: NRO (for Indian income - rent, dividend, interest), NRE (for foreign source - repatriable), FCNR (foreign currency fixed deposit, repatriable). Not regular savings (that's for residents).
  • Indian property: OCI can purchase residential / commercial property (not agricultural). Rental income taxable in India (standard deduction 30% + municipal tax + interest on home loan deduction).
  • Indian mutual fund / shares: via NRO or NRE demat. Capital gain on sale: LTCG 12.5% (post Finance Act 2024) or STCG 20% for listed equity. Interest / dividend taxable.
  • Indian ITR filing: mandatory if India-source income exceeds basic exemption (currently INR 3 lakh for NRIs). ITR-2 or ITR-3 depending on nature.
  • Form 10F (Form 41 from 1 April 2026) + TRC: for DTAA treaty rate claim. See Form 10F guide.
  • Section 54 (section 82 of the 2025 Act) reinvestment: available on sale of Indian property (same rules as resident).
  • Repatriation: up to USD 1M/year from NRO under FEMA; unlimited from NRE / FCNR.

/ Specific OCI complications in 2026

Current traps.

  • PAN requirement: OCI typically needs Indian PAN for ITR filing, property purchase, financial transactions above INR 2.5 lakh. Apply via Form 49AA (NRI PAN).
  • PAN-Aadhaar linking: NRIs are EXEMPT from mandatory PAN-Aadhaar linking (Notification 37/2017). OCI should ensure PAN is marked NRI in database. See our PAN-Aadhaar guide.
  • Deemed resident under Section 6(1A): Indian citizen or OCI stay in India > 120 days (if India income > INR 15 lakh) treated as deemed resident but RNOR — narrow trap for high-income OCIs with substantial Indian income.
  • US estate tax: if OCI is US tax person, worldwide assets (including Indian) in US estate. See US estate tax guide.
  • FBAR + Form 8938: US tax person with foreign financial accounts must report. India NRO / NRE / MF holdings disclosable.
  • LRS on Indian income: cannot use LRS as OCI (LRS is for Indian residents only). Repatriation of Indian income via NRO - USD 1M/year route instead.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Does OCI card make me an Indian tax resident?
No - OCI is an immigration document, not a tax status. Indian tax residence is determined solely by Section 6 Income Tax Act physical presence tests (182+ days in FY, or 60+ days + 365+ days in preceding 4 FYs). Most OCI holders residing abroad maintain NRI status; only short visitors to India stay NRI by default.
What is deemed resident under Section 6(1A) for OCI holder?
Finance Act 2020 inserted Section 6(1A): Indian citizen or OCI who stays in India for more than 120 days (if India-source income > INR 15 lakh) is treated as deemed resident but with Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) status. Applies only to high-India-income OCIs - most ordinary OCI holders unaffected. Benefit: can be in India longer without triggering full Resident status, but with RNOR tax treatment.
Can OCI holder buy agricultural land in India?
No - OCI cannot buy agricultural / plantation / farmhouse. Can inherit (same as any NRI). Can purchase residential / commercial property freely. For agricultural land purchase, OCI must have RBI prior approval (rarely granted).
Does OCI holder need to link PAN with Aadhaar?
No - NRIs including OCI holders are EXEMPT from mandatory PAN-Aadhaar linking under CBDT Notification 37/2017. However, PAN database must correctly reflect NRI status; if PAN was issued when holder was resident and status changed to NRI, update required. Common issue - PAN marked as resident but holder is actually OCI/NRI results in PAN inoperative notice.
Does OCI holder need to file Indian ITR?
Only if India-source income exceeds basic exemption limit (currently INR 3 lakh for NRIs under old regime; INR 7 lakh income under new regime with Section 87A [section 156 of the 2025 Act] rebate). Income types that typically trigger ITR: Indian property rent, Indian capital gains, Indian bank interest above INR 10K, India salary. Even where ITR not mandatory, filing often beneficial to claim Section 195 TDS refund.
