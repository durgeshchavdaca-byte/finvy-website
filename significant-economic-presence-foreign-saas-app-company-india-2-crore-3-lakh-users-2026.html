/ Foreign companies · Digital tax · Income-tax Act 2025 · Updated 2026-11-14

Foreign SaaS, App and Online Companies Earning From India in 2026: Significant Economic Presence, the INR 2 Crore and 3 Lakh-User Tests, and Treaty Protection, no office in India needed to be taxed.

A foreign company with no office, staff or server in India can still have a taxable business connection in India through significant economic presence (SEP): either payments from India above INR 2 crore in a year for goods, services or property (including data and software downloads), or systematic and continuous dealings with 3 lakh or more users in India. The rule is in section 9 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026), carried over from the 1961 Act. Only income attributable to India is taxed, and a company resident in a treaty country such as the US, UK, Singapore, UAE, Canada or Australia is usually protected by the treaty's permanent establishment test, provided it can produce a tax residency certificate and Form 10F (now Form 41).

/ The two tests

Either one creates SEP.

TestThresholdWhat counts
Payment (revenue) testAggregate payments above INR 2 crore in the tax yearTransactions in goods, services or property with any person in India, including download of data or software
User test3 lakh or more users in IndiaSystematic and continuous soliciting of business or interaction with users in India through digital means

SEP applies whether or not the contract is signed in India, the company has a place of business in India, or the services are rendered in India. The thresholds were first set in rule 11UD (May 2021) and continue under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Buying goods in India only for export does not create SEP.

/ Are you actually taxable?

Treaty country or not decides most cases.

Your situationUsual position
Resident in a treaty country (US, UK, Singapore, UAE, Canada, Australia and others), no permanent establishment in India, TRC and Form 10F/41 availableTreaty prevails where more beneficial: business profits taxed in India only if there is a PE, so SEP alone usually creates no Indian income tax
Treaty country, but India-based staff, dependent agents or a fixed place in IndiaPE risk: profits attributable to the PE are taxed in India (foreign company rate 35% plus surcharge and cess)
Resident in a country with no tax treaty with India, or treaty benefit denied (no TRC, principal purpose test)SEP applies in full; income attributable to Indian revenue and users is taxable and a return is needed
Below both thresholdsNo SEP; check other rules (royalty or fees for technical services, PE) separately

The treaty is your shield, but only if you can show it.

Indian customers and the tax department will ask for a tax residency certificate and Form 10F (now Form 41) before applying treaty rates. Keep them ready every year, and keep India-facing staff and decision-making from creating a permanent establishment. WhatsApp CA Durgesh Chavda.

/ What income is taxed

Only the India-attributable part.

  • Only income reasonably attributable to the Indian transactions or users is taxed, not global profit.
  • This expressly includes income from advertising targeted at Indian customers or accessed through Indian IP addresses, sale of data collected from India, and goods or services sold using such data.
  • The equalisation levy no longer shelters this income: the 2% levy on e-commerce supplies ended on 1 August 2024 and the 6% levy on online advertising ended on 1 April 2025, so these receipts are now tested under the income-tax rules instead.

/ The other two Indian taxes on the same revenue

Withholding and GST.

  • Withholding by Indian business customers: an Indian payer must deduct tax on payments to a non-resident if the income is chargeable in India, and certify remittances (Forms 145/146, formerly 15CA/15CB). For standard software licences the Supreme Court held in Engineering Analysis (2 March 2021) that the payment is not royalty under the treaties, which many customers rely on with your TRC and Form 10F.
  • GST on online services (OIDAR): selling digital services to consumers in India (B2C) requires simplified GST registration and IGST at 18%, even with no Indian presence; for B2B sales the Indian business pays GST under reverse charge. See OIDAR GST for foreign SaaS.
  • Data rules: the DPDP Act applies to processing personal data of people in India; see DPDP for foreign companies.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is significant economic presence in Indian tax?
A rule under which a non-resident has a business connection in India, without any office or staff here, if payments from India for goods, services or property (including data or software downloads) exceed INR 2 crore in a year, or if it systematically and continuously deals with 3 lakh or more users in India.
Does SEP apply under the new Income-tax Act, 2025?
Yes. The concept is in section 9 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026, with the INR 2 crore and 3 lakh user thresholds continued in the Income-tax Rules, 2026.
Our SaaS company is in the US with no Indian office. Do we pay Indian income tax?
Usually not on business profits, if you are entitled to the India-US treaty and have no permanent establishment in India. The treaty prevails where it is more beneficial, but you need a tax residency certificate and Form 10F (now Form 41) to claim it.
Which companies are fully exposed to SEP?
Companies resident in countries without a tax treaty with India, those that cannot show treaty residence, and those denied treaty benefits, for example under the principal purpose test.
Is the equalisation levy still charged?
No. The 2% levy on e-commerce supplies ended on 1 August 2024 and the 6% levy on online advertising ended on 1 April 2025. Those receipts are now tested under the income-tax rules, including SEP.
Do we need GST registration in India?
If you sell online services to consumers in India, yes: simplified OIDAR registration and IGST at 18%. For sales to Indian businesses, the customer pays GST under reverse charge.
