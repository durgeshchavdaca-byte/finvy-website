Ind AS vs IFRS (2026): Key Differences and Carve-Outs, When Ind AS Applies, and What Foreign Parents Need From Their Indian Subsidiary, close to IFRS, but not identical.

Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) are converged with IFRS, but they include carve-outs and carve-ins for Indian law and practice - so Ind AS financial statements may not be fully IFRS-compliant. Ind AS is mandatory for listed companies, unlisted companies with net worth of INR 250 crore or more, and their group companies; other companies use the older Accounting Standards (Indian GAAP). A foreign parent reporting under IFRS usually needs a reconciliation or a group reporting pack from its Indian subsidiary.

/ Applicability and group reporting

Who uses Ind AS, and what the parent needs.

  • Mandatory Ind AS: listed companies and companies in the process of listing, unlisted companies with net worth of INR 250 crore or more, and their holding, subsidiary, joint venture and associate companies (Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015).
  • Indian subsidiary of a foreign company: applies Ind AS if it meets these criteria itself or chooses to; otherwise it reports under Indian GAAP.
  • Once adopted: a company that moves to Ind AS continues with it.
  • Group reporting pack: the Indian statutory accounts, and a GAAP-difference reconciliation to IFRS (or US GAAP) for consolidation.
  • Calendar mismatch: Indian companies use an April-March financial year; groups with calendar-year reporting need interim cut-offs.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the difference between Ind AS and IFRS?
Ind AS are India's converged version of IFRS, with carve-outs and carve-ins for Indian law and practice. For example, Ind AS 40 allows only the cost model for investment property, and Ind AS 103 sends a bargain purchase gain to capital reserve rather than profit or loss. As a result, Ind AS financial statements may not fully comply with IFRS.
Which companies must follow Ind AS in India?
Listed companies and those in the process of listing, unlisted companies with net worth of INR 250 crore or more, and their holding, subsidiary, joint venture and associate companies. Others may adopt Ind AS voluntarily; the rest follow the Accounting Standards (Indian GAAP).
Does an Indian subsidiary of a foreign company have to use Ind AS?
Only if it meets the Ind AS criteria itself or chooses to adopt it. Otherwise it prepares statutory accounts under Indian GAAP and gives the parent a reconciliation for consolidation.
Are Ind AS financial statements IFRS-compliant?
Not necessarily. Because of carve-outs, Ind AS statements may not fully comply with IFRS, so foreign parents and investors often ask for a reconciliation.
What does a foreign parent need from its Indian subsidiary at year end?
The Indian statutory financial statements and a group reporting pack adjusted to the group's framework, covering GAAP differences and any April-March to calendar-year cut-off.
