/ NRI Guide · US Estate Tax · Updated 2026-10-15

US estate tax on Indian assets, what NRIs often miss.

US citizen or green card holder dies with Indian property / Indian company shares / Indian bank balance — those assets are included in US estate for US estate tax. USD 13.99M lifetime exemption (2026) is generous but TCJA sunset reduces it to ~USD 7M in 2026 if Congress doesn't act. No India-US estate tax treaty means potential double tax. Here's the complete 2026 planning framework.

/ The US estate tax reach

Why it matters.

  • US estate tax (IRC Section 2001-2210): taxes worldwide gross estate of US citizen / US domiciliary at death at graduated rates up to 40%.
  • US citizens: worldwide assets taxed. No geographic limit.
  • US green card holders (LPR, domiciliaries): worldwide assets taxed if domiciled in US. Domicile = physical presence + intent to remain permanently.
  • Non-US-domiciled aliens (NRIs without green card): only US-situs assets taxed (US real estate, US company shares, US tangibles in US).
  • Lifetime exemption 2026: USD 13.99 million per person (indexed). TCJA provision — sunsets end of 2025 to ~USD 7 million (2026 inflation-adjusted ~USD 7-7.5M) unless Congress extends. Significant uncertainty.
  • Rate: 40% on amounts above exemption.
  • Form 706: US Estate Tax Return. Filed within 9 months of death (6-month extension possible).

/ Indian assets in US estate

What's included.

  • Indian real estate: residential + commercial property. Valued at fair market value on date of death.
  • Indian company shares: listed + unlisted. Listed at NSE/BSE closing price; unlisted at FMV (DCF or NAV).
  • Indian mutual fund + ETF holdings.
  • Indian bank balances: NRE, NRO, FCNR — all at date-of-death balance (converted at date-of-death FX rate).
  • Indian partnership + LLP interests.
  • Indian provident fund + superannuation balances (complex — depends on fund type).
  • Indian insurance policy proceeds payable to estate (not payable to named beneficiary — those bypass estate).
  • Indian jewellery / valuables: Indian location + Indian valuation.

All added to US estate at FX-converted fair market value as of date of death.

/ No India-US estate tax treaty

The double-tax risk.

  • India abolished estate duty in 1985 — no Indian estate tax.
  • US estate tax does apply to US citizens / domiciliaries on worldwide assets including Indian.
  • No India-US estate tax treaty means no formal mechanism to avoid double tax (though double tax is unlikely because India has no estate tax).
  • Transfer stamp duty in India: when Indian property transfers to heirs post-death, stamp duty + registration applicable in India (varies by state, typically 2-7% of circle rate). Not a 'tax' per se but a real cost.
  • Capital gain at future sale: Indian heirs who later sell inherited property pay Indian capital gain tax on sale. Cost of acquisition for them = cost in NRI's hands + indexation (not FMV at death). Important: no Indian step-up at death (unlike US).

/ Planning strategies

Reducing exposure.

  • Annual gift exclusion: USD 19,000 per donee per year (2026). Gift Indian assets gradually to US-citizen spouse or US-citizen children. Reduces estate while using annual exclusion.
  • Spousal unlimited marital deduction: unlimited for US-citizen spouse. Transfer all Indian assets to US-citizen spouse via Will — no estate tax on first death. Second-to-die planning needed.
  • QDOT (Qualified Domestic Trust): for non-US-citizen spouse, standard marital deduction not available. QDOT structure (US trust with US trustee) qualifies for marital deduction. Specific IRC Section 2056A rules.
  • Credit shelter trust: at first death, apply USD 13.99M exemption to a trust that grows outside survivor's estate. Standard US estate planning.
  • Lifetime gifts of Indian assets: value appreciation after gift stays with donee — reduces estate growth. Caution: gift tax applies to gifts above annual exclusion; uses lifetime unified exemption.
  • Life insurance for liquidity: Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT) holds policy — proceeds outside estate.
  • Charitable giving: unlimited charitable deduction. Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT) can defer / reduce tax.
  • Pre-mortem planning for Indian property: joint ownership with US-citizen spouse / children can shift fractional interests out of estate. Must comply with Indian stamp duty + mutation.
  • Non-resident alien route: if green card holder willing to expatriate (Section 877A exit tax applies), post-expatriation only US-situs assets in US estate. Indian assets outside US estate.

/ TCJA sunset risk

What to watch.

  • Current USD 13.99M exemption (2026) is temporary — set by TCJA 2017 to double the pre-2017 exemption. Scheduled to sunset end of 2025 (2026 fiscal year start) to roughly half (~USD 7M in 2026 inflation-adjusted terms).
  • Congress may extend / modify in 2025-2026 tax legislation. Significant political uncertainty.
  • Planning action: NRI with large estate (above ~USD 7M including Indian assets) should consider using current USD 13.99M exemption via lifetime gifting + trust structuring BEFORE potential sunset.
  • Clawback rule: IRS has stated that gifts using 2017-2025 doubled exemption will NOT be clawed back after 2025 sunset. 'Use it or lose it' planning advantageous.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Do my Indian assets face US estate tax if I'm a US citizen or green card holder?
Yes. US citizens are taxed on worldwide gross estate at death; same for US-domiciled green card holders. Indian real estate, Indian company shares, Indian bank balances, Indian mutual funds all included in US estate at FX-converted fair market value on date of death. Lifetime exemption USD 13.99M (2026) - amount above exempted threshold taxed at up to 40%.
What happens when TCJA exemption sunsets in 2026?
Current USD 13.99M lifetime exemption is scheduled to roughly halve to ~USD 7M in 2026 (TCJA sunset). Congress may extend / modify. If sunset occurs, estate tax exposure increases materially for mid-wealthy NRI estates. Planning action: use current USD 13.99M via lifetime gifting + trust structuring BEFORE sunset. IRS has confirmed no clawback for gifts made at higher exemption.
Is there an India-US estate tax treaty?
No - India abolished estate duty in 1985 and has no estate tax. US estate tax applies to US citizens / domiciliaries on worldwide assets including Indian. No treaty mechanism exists but double tax is practically avoided because India has no estate tax. However: Indian stamp duty on heir mutation (2-7% of circle rate) applies; Indian capital gain on heir's later sale uses NRI's cost basis (no Indian step-up at death).
Can I give my Indian property to my US-citizen spouse to avoid estate tax?
Yes - unlimited marital deduction for US-citizen spouse. Transfer Indian property to US-citizen spouse via lifetime gift or Will - no US estate tax on first death. Second-to-die planning needed for ultimate exposure. If spouse is NOT US citizen, standard marital deduction unavailable - use QDOT (Qualified Domestic Trust) structure under IRC Section 2056A to qualify.
What is QDOT and when do I need one?
Qualified Domestic Trust - US trust with US trustee holding assets for benefit of non-US-citizen spouse. Qualifies for US estate tax marital deduction (which is otherwise unavailable for non-citizen spouse under IRC Section 2056(d)). Specific rules: US trustee + distribution restrictions. Used by US citizen/green card holder who dies with non-US-citizen spouse, especially NRI couples where one has US status and other doesn't.
