Section 8 Company in India (2026): What It Is, Registration, Licence, Compliances After Incorporation, 12A and 80G, CSR-1 and Annual Compliance, a non-profit with the structure of a company.

A Section 8 company is a non-profit company under section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013, formed to promote objects such as charity, education, science, art, sports, social welfare, religion or environmental protection. It must apply its income only to those objects and cannot pay dividends to members; breaching the conditions can lead to the licence being revoked. It is incorporated on the MCA portal with a licence under section 8, needs no minimum capital, and can drop “Limited” from its name. For tax exemption and donor deductions it registers under sections 12A and 80G, and to take CSR money as an implementing agency it also registers on the MCA CSR-1 form.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • What is a Section 8 company? (Section 8 company kya hoti hai?) A company formed for charitable or other public-benefit objects that cannot distribute profits to its members.
  • Is there a minimum capital? No. Minimum paid-up capital for companies was removed in 2015.
  • How many directors and members? A private Section 8 company needs at least two directors and two members.
  • Can it make a profit? It can earn a surplus, but must use it for its objects; no dividend can be paid.
  • Is it tax-exempt automatically? No. Exemption needs registration under section 12A, and donors get a deduction only if it also has 80G registration.
  • What financial statements does it prepare? Full financial statements under Schedule III of the Companies Act, audited by a chartered accountant, plus the income and expenditure presentation required for a non-profit.

/ Registration and compliance

From licence to annual filings.

  • Incorporation: reserve the name, draft the memorandum and articles with non-profit objects, and file the incorporation and section 8 licence application on the MCA portal. A separate Form INC-12 is used to convert an existing company into a Section 8 company.
  • Tax registrations: apply for 12A (income exemption) and 80G (donor deduction) after incorporation; provisional registration is granted first and must later be converted to regular registration.
  • CSR: to receive CSR funds as an implementing agency, register on the MCA CSR-1 form, which needs 12A and 80G; see CSR under section 135.
  • Annual compliance: statutory audit, AOC-4 (financial statements) and MGT-7 (annual return) with the Registrar, the income tax return claiming exemption, board and general meetings, and DIR-3 KYC for directors once every three years.
  • Foreign money: foreign contributions need FCRA registration or prior permission before they are received.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a Section 8 company?
A non-profit company registered under section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 to promote objects such as charity, education, science, art, sports, social welfare or environmental protection. It must apply its income to those objects and cannot pay dividends to its members.
What is the minimum capital for a Section 8 company?
There is no minimum paid-up capital requirement. A private Section 8 company needs at least two directors and two members.
Is a Section 8 company exempt from income tax?
Only after it registers under section 12AB (section 332 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026) and meets the conditions for charitable exemption. Donors can claim a deduction only if it also holds 80G approval (now section 354).
Can a Section 8 company receive CSR funds?
Yes, as a CSR implementing agency, if it registers on the MCA CSR-1 form, which requires 12A and 80G registration.
Which NGOs are eligible for CSR funding?
Under the CSR rules, a company can spend CSR funds through a Section 8 company, registered public trust or registered society that is registered under section 12A and approved under section 80G, provided it has a track record of at least three years in similar activities - unless it was set up by the company itself or by a government. Every such implementing agency must also register with the Central Government in Form CSR-1 before receiving CSR funds.
What is the difference between a trust, a society and a Section 8 company?
A trust is set up by a trust deed and run by trustees, under state public trust laws where they apply. A society is registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or a state act by at least seven members and run by an elected committee. A Section 8 company is incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 with a licence, files annual returns with the Registrar and has the most formal governance, which corporate and foreign donors often prefer. All three can obtain 12A and 80G registration, CSR-1 registration and, where needed, FCRA registration.
What annual filings does a Section 8 company have?
Audited financial statements in AOC-4 and the annual return in MGT-7 with the Registrar, the income tax return, board and general meetings, and director KYC. Foreign contributions need FCRA registration or permission.
What are the compliances for a section 8 company after incorporation?
In the first months: hold the first board meeting within 30 days of incorporation; the board appoints the first auditor within 30 days (the auditor's ADT-1 is filed); a section 8 company with share capital files the INC-20A declaration of commencement of business within 180 days, after the subscribers have paid for their shares (one limited by guarantee without share capital does not); issue share certificates and set up the statutory registers; open the bank account and register for GST, professional tax or other laws only where they apply. For tax, apply for provisional registration under section 12AB (section 332 of the Income-tax Act, 2025) and 80G (section 354) - without them the company is taxed like any other and donors get no deduction - then CSR-1 to receive CSR funds, and FCRA registration or prior permission before accepting foreign contributions. Every year: board meetings, the AGM, audited accounts in AOC-4 and the annual return in MGT-7 / 7A, director KYC every three years, the income tax return with the audit report, and keeping to the licence conditions - income applied only to the objects and no dividend or profit to members.
