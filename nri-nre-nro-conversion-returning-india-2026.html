NRE / NRO / FCNR accounts, converting when you return.

NRI returning to settle in India must convert NRE / NRO / FCNR accounts to resident accounts within reasonable time of becoming resident under FEMA. Options: Regular Savings, RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account, FCNR-B conversion to RFC at maturity. Each has different tax + repatriation implications. Mishandled: FEMA breach + continuing to use NRE as resident risks compounding. Here's the complete transition framework.

/ The trigger - when must conversion happen

FEMA residence shift.

  • FEMA residence: distinct from Income Tax residence. FEMA Section 2(v) defines 'person resident in India' broadly — stays in India for more than 182 days during preceding FY OR intends to come to India for employment / business / stay for uncertain period.
  • Trigger of residence: once NRI's intention to stay in India for uncertain period crystallises (job in India, settlement plan, bringing family), FEMA residence may trigger even before 182 days physical presence.
  • Conversion obligation: 'within reasonable time' of becoming resident, NRE / NRO / FCNR accounts must be converted / designated as resident. 'Reasonable time' interpreted by banks as 60-90 days typical but not strictly defined.
  • Continuing to operate NRE / NRO as resident = FEMA breach. Prolonged breach can trigger compounding under Section 13.

/ Account options post-return

What to convert to.

1. Regular Savings Account

  • Standard rupee-denominated resident account.
  • NRO account typically re-designated as regular savings. Same account number, different flag.
  • Interest fully taxable at slab rate.
  • Form 15G / 15H can be submitted to avoid TDS where applicable.

2. RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) Account

  • Available only to Indian residents who were previously NRIs for a continuous period of 1+ year.
  • Denominated in: USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, CAD, AUD + others.
  • Fully repatriable — funds can be remitted abroad freely for any permissible purpose.
  • Interest taxable in India at slab rate (foreign-source but received in India).
  • No cap on balance.
  • Typically used by returning NRIs who may re-expatriate; preserves foreign currency.
  • Source of funds: NRE / FCNR maturity proceeds (transferable); foreign currency brought into India during return move; pension / superannuation from foreign employer.

3. FCNR-B account (maturity)

  • Existing FCNR-B deposit (foreign currency fixed deposit opened as NRI) can continue until maturity.
  • At maturity, must be transferred to RFC account (not auto-renewable as FCNR-B once resident).
  • Interest tax-exempt until maturity (per earlier NRI status rules).

4. RFC(D) Deposit (Resident Foreign Currency Domestic)

  • For Indian residents who weren't previously NRI (e.g., forex gift receipt, pension from foreign source).
  • Non-repatriable.
  • Different from RFC — chosen by never-NRI residents.

/ Transition workflow

Step by step.

  1. Determine FEMA residence trigger date: when did you become resident under FEMA? Typically arrival + settled intention date.
  2. Notify bank: visit NRE / NRO / FCNR bank with change-of-status declaration + supporting documents (Indian address proof, employment letter, flight ticket).
  3. NRO account: typically re-designated as regular savings. Same account number. Interest from NRO continues; TDS at resident rate (10% without Form 15G/H).
  4. NRE account: must be closed or converted. Options:
  5.   — Transfer balance to Regular Savings (if NRE funds are repatriable, they lose that status once converted).
  6.   — Transfer to RFC (preserves foreign currency + repatriable status).
  7.   — Early-close NRE and remit funds abroad before becoming resident.
  8. FCNR deposit: continue until maturity; then transfer to RFC. Early-close is penalty-chargeable.
  9. PIS (Portfolio Investment Scheme) account: close / transfer securities to resident demat.
  10. Mutual fund + demat KYC: update to resident. Change of address + bank account linking.
  11. Insurance policies: notify insurer of status change.
  12. Credit card + loan accounts: notify of status change.

/ Tax treatment transition

The RNOR optimisation.

  • Returning NRI typically gets RNOR status for 2-3 years post-return (per Section 6(6) — see RNOR guide).
  • During RNOR: foreign-source investment income + overseas rental + foreign capital gain remain non-taxable in India. Only India-source + foreign business controlled from India + foreign profession set up in India are taxable.
  • RFC interest: taxable in India (received in India) — even during RNOR.
  • NRE interest: during RNOR, interest from NRE / FCNR period before residence trigger remains tax-exempt (as it accrued during NRI status). Interest on balance after FEMA residence trigger is taxable even if account is still technically NRE.
  • Full resident status (post RNOR, typically year 4): global income taxable. Foreign Tax Credit (Section 90/91) available.
  • Planning: during RNOR window, consolidate foreign assets, repatriate large amounts (while tax-free in India via RNOR exemption), structure holdings to minimise post-RNOR tax hit.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

When do I need to convert my NRE/NRO account after returning to India?
Within reasonable time of becoming FEMA resident - typically 60-90 days post-arrival with settled intention to stay. FEMA residence (Section 2(v)) is distinct from Income Tax residence and can trigger even before 182 days physical presence if intention + employment / business set up suggests settlement. Prolonged operation of NRE/NRO as resident = FEMA breach + potential compounding under Section 13.
What is RFC account and should I open one?
RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account - foreign currency-denominated account for Indian residents who were NRI for 1+ year. Preserves foreign currency + fully repatriable + no balance limit. Interest taxable in India. Useful if you may re-expatriate, receive foreign pension, hold foreign currency savings. Alternative to converting NRE to Regular Savings (which loses foreign currency + repatriable status).
What happens to my FCNR deposit when I return?
FCNR-B deposit continues until maturity (interest tax-exempt during NRI status period preserved). At maturity, cannot renew as FCNR-B (only NRIs can hold). Transfer to RFC account to preserve foreign currency denomination. Early closure is penalty-chargeable - avoid unless urgent.
Will my NRE interest be taxable after I return to India?
Interest accrued during NRI period (before FEMA residence trigger): tax-exempt. Interest accrued after becoming FEMA resident: taxable even if account is still technically NRE. Convert NRE promptly after returning; keeping NRE operational post-FEMA-residence doesn't give you tax exemption and risks FEMA breach.
Can I keep some money in NRE after I return?
Technically no - FEMA requires NRE conversion within reasonable time of becoming resident. Practical workaround: convert NRE balance to RFC (preserves foreign currency + repatriable status). RFC is specifically designed for returning NRIs who want to retain foreign currency position.
