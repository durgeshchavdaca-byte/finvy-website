/ Multi-state businesses, foreign-owned groups and GCCs with several GST registrations · Distinct persons · Updated 2026-11-29

GST Cross Charge Between Head Office and Branches (2026): Circular 199/11/2023, Valuation, Internally Generated Services and Mandatory ISD From 1 April 2025, third-party costs through ISD, internal services by cross charge.

Each GST registration of a business is a separate ‘distinct person’, so services between the head office and branches in other states are taxable supplies even without payment. Circular 199/11/2023 settled valuation: where the receiving branch can take full credit, the value on the invoice - or nil if no invoice is raised for internal services - is accepted. From 1 April 2025, credit on common services bought from third parties must be distributed through an Input Service Distributor (ISD) registration instead of being cross-charged.

/ Cross charge vs ISD

Who uses which route.

SituationRoute
Common services bought from third parties (rent, software, audit) used by several registrationsDistribute credit through an ISD registration - mandatory from 1 April 2025
Internally generated services (head office teams serving branches)Cross charge by tax invoice where applicable
Recipient branch eligible for full input tax creditValue declared on the invoice accepted as open market value; nil if no invoice for internal services (Circular 199/11/2023)
Recipient branch not eligible for full credit (exempt or blocked supplies)Value under rule 28 - open market value, like-kind value or 110% of cost
  • Distinct persons: registrations of the same PAN in different states are distinct persons under section 25(4), and Schedule I treats supplies between them as taxable even without consideration.
  • Salary costs: employees are not separately supplying services to their employer, and the circular addresses how internal services are valued for full-credit recipients.

/ Practical compliance

Set it up once, review yearly.

  • ISD registration: needed where third-party invoices for common services are received at one office but used by several registrations.
  • Distribution: the ISD distributes credit to the registrations that use the services, in the prescribed ratio, through monthly ISD returns.
  • Exempt or partly exempt branches: cross-charge valuation matters most here, because tax on the cross charge becomes a cost.
  • Foreign-owned groups and GCCs: intercompany charges from the foreign parent are imports of services under reverse charge - separate from branch cross charges.
  • Audits and notices: departments test missing ISD registrations and unreported cross charges, so document the policy and the allocation basis.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is cross charge in GST?
A tax invoice raised by one GST registration of a business, usually the head office, on another registration of the same business in a different state for services it provides, such as management or IT support. The registrations are distinct persons, so the supply is taxable even without payment.
What did Circular 199/11/2023 clarify on cross charge?
Where the receiving branch is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared on the head office's invoice is treated as the open market value, and for internally generated services the value can be nil if no invoice is raised. Where the branch cannot take full credit, valuation follows rule 28.
Is ISD mandatory under GST?
Yes, from 1 April 2025. Credit on common input services bought from third parties for use by more than one registration must be distributed through an Input Service Distributor registration (amended sections 2(61) and 20, Notification 16/2024-Central Tax).
What is the difference between ISD and cross charge?
ISD distributes credit on services bought from third parties; cross charge taxes services the business provides internally from one registration to another.
Does cross charge apply to services from a foreign parent company?
No. Charges from a foreign parent are imports of services, taxed under reverse charge by the Indian entity - a separate issue from cross charges between Indian branches.
