For your review.

  • Business returns: Forms 1065, 1120-S and 1120, with K-1s; Form 5472 and pro forma 1120 for foreign-owned LLCs.

/ How it works

Built around your review process.

  1. Scoping call and a sample set of returns or files.
  2. Engagement letter and confidentiality agreement; your firm updates its client engagement letters and collects Section 7216 consents.
  3. Access through your systems (remote desktop or your cloud tax software) so data stays in your environment where possible.
  4. We prepare; your reviewer reviews, and your firm signs and files.
  5. Review notes and a turnaround schedule agreed for each season.

/ Compliance you need in place

Before any return leaves your office.

  • IRC Section 7216 consent: before tax return information goes to a preparer outside the United States, the taxpayer must sign a separate, dated consent. For Form 1040-series returns, Rev. Proc. 2013-14 prescribes the wording.
  • Social Security numbers: a full SSN can go offshore only if the consent specifically allows it; otherwise it is redacted before files leave your firm.
  • Duration: a consent that names no period lasts one year; give the client a copy when it is signed.
  • AICPA: the Code's interpretation on using a third-party service provider requires you to tell clients, before sharing confidential information, that a service provider may be used (an engagement-letter clause works), and to have a confidentiality agreement with the provider.
  • FTC Safeguards Rule: tax and accounting firms need a written information security plan (IRS Publication 5708 is a template) that covers service providers. A breach of unencrypted data affecting 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days.
  • Signing: your firm reviews and signs the return and files it from your own e-file account; we prepare under your review.

Sources: IRS Rev. Proc. 2013-14; CPA Journal: outsourcing tax preparation overseas; Journal of Accountancy: third-party service providers; IRS Publication 5708 (WISP template).

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Can a US CPA firm outsource tax return preparation to India?
Yes, if the taxpayer first signs a Section 7216 consent to disclose return information to a preparer outside the United States, the firm tells clients it uses a third-party service provider, and the provider is covered by the firm's confidentiality agreement and written information security plan. The US firm reviews, signs and files the return.
Which returns can you prepare?
Forms 1040, 1065, 1120-S, 1120 and 1099s, plus Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs, along with bookkeeping, reconciliations and year-end workpapers.
Which software do you use?
Your firm's tax software and your clients' accounting systems, such as QuickBooks Online or Xero, accessed through your environment.
Does data leave our systems?
It does not have to. We can work entirely on your servers or cloud software through remote access, which keeps files in your environment.
