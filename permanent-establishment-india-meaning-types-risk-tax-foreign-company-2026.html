Permanent Establishment (PE) in India (2026): Meaning, the Four Types, How Foreign Companies Trigger It and What It Costs, where a foreign company becomes taxable in India.

A permanent establishment (PE) is the taxable presence a foreign company creates in India under a tax treaty. Once there is a PE, India taxes the profits attributable to it at the foreign company rate of 35% plus surcharge and cess, and the company must file Indian returns and keep transfer pricing documentation. Treaties recognise four main types: a fixed place (office, branch, site at the company's disposal), a service PE (staff providing services in India beyond a time threshold), a construction or installation PE, and a dependent agent PE (someone in India habitually concluding contracts or playing the principal role in them). Without a treaty, India's broader domestic 'business connection' test applies, including significant economic presence.

/ The four types of PE

And what usually triggers them.

TypeTypical triggerLeading case
Fixed place PEPremises in India at the foreign company's disposal through which its business is carried on, even without a formal officeFormula One World Championship (Supreme Court, 2017)
Service PEEmployees or personnel furnishing services in India beyond the treaty's day threshold within 12 monthsMorgan Stanley (Supreme Court, 2007)
Construction or installation PEA building site or installation project lasting longer than the treaty periodTreaty-specific thresholds
Dependent agent PEA person in India habitually concluding contracts, or playing the principal role leading to them, for the foreign companyWidened by the MLI where India's treaty partner adopted it

Back-office support that is merely preparatory or auxiliary generally does not create a PE; the Supreme Court in E-Funds (2017) found no PE where the Indian subsidiary performed back-office services for its US parent.

/ How foreign companies trigger PE by accident

Common situations.

  • Senior staff of the foreign company working from India for long periods, or remote employees who negotiate and close deals; see PE risk from remote employees.
  • Seconded employees whose work is controlled by the foreign parent rather than the Indian company.
  • Indian distributors or sales agents who effectively sign contracts for the foreign company.
  • Project teams on site in India beyond the treaty period.
  • Using an Indian subsidiary's premises as the parent's own office.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is a permanent establishment in India?
The taxable presence a foreign company creates in India under a tax treaty, for example an office, staff providing services beyond a time threshold, a long construction project, or a dependent agent concluding contracts.
What tax applies if a foreign company has a PE in India?
Profits attributable to the PE are taxed at 35% plus surcharge and cess, and the company must file Indian returns.
Can remote employees in India create a PE?
Yes, especially if they negotiate or conclude contracts, work from a fixed place for long periods, or provide services beyond the treaty threshold.
Does an Indian subsidiary create a PE for its parent?
Not by itself. A subsidiary is a separate taxpayer; a PE can arise if the parent uses the subsidiary's premises or staff as its own, but back-office services alone did not create a PE in E-Funds (2017).
What is a service PE?
Under many Indian treaties, a PE arising when a foreign company's employees or personnel furnish services in India beyond a specified number of days within a 12-month period.
