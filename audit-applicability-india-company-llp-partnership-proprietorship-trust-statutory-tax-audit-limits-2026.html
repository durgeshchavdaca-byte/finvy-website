Audit Applicability in India (2026): Company, LLP, Partnership Firm, Proprietorship and Trust - Statutory Audit vs Tax Audit Limits, which audit, and from what turnover.

A company always needs a statutory audit, whatever its size. An LLP needs one only when its turnover exceeds Rs 40 lakh or partners' contribution exceeds Rs 25 lakh. Partnership firms and proprietors have no statutory audit, but any business crossing the income tax limits - Rs 1 crore turnover, or Rs 10 crore when cash dealings are within 5%, and Rs 50 lakh for professionals - needs a tax audit. Trusts claiming exemption need their accounts audited once their income before exemption crosses the basic exemption limit. Only a chartered accountant can sign any of these audits.

/ Audit applicability by entity

One table, five entity types.

Partnership firmNone under the Partnership ActWhen turnover or receipts cross the 44AB limits, or presumptive income is declared below the scheme rate
ProprietorshipNoneSame as a firm - on the proprietor's business turnover or professional receipts
Trust or institution claiming exemptionAccounts audited when income before exemption exceeds the basic exemption limit (Form 10B / 10BB under the 1961 rules)Covered by the trust audit report instead
  • Tax audit limits: business turnover above Rs 1 crore, or above Rs 10 crore where cash receipts and cash payments are each within 5% of the total; professional gross receipts above Rs 50 lakh.
  • Presumptive scheme: businesses under section 44AD and professionals under section 44ADA who declare at least the scheme rate do not need a tax audit; declaring less, with income above the basic exemption limit, brings it in.
  • GST: there is no separate GST audit by a CA now - the annual return GSTR-9 and the self-certified reconciliation GSTR-9C are filed by the taxpayer.

/ Deadlines and what goes wrong

Dates and the cost of missing them.

  • Company: the auditor is appointed within 30 days of incorporation for the first auditor and at the AGM after that (Form ADT-1); audited accounts are filed in AOC-4 within 30 days of the AGM.
  • LLP: audited accounts go into Form 8 by 30 October; the annual return Form 11 is due by 30 May.
  • Tax audit: the audit report is due one month before the return due date for audit cases - check the year's dates, which are sometimes extended by CBDT circular.
  • Missing a tax audit can draw a penalty under section 271B (0.5% of turnover, capped), and a late company or LLP filing runs up additional fees for each day of delay.
  • Trust audit missed can cost the exemption for that year, so the report must be filed before the return.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is audit compulsory for every company in India?
Yes. Every company - private or public, with or without turnover - must have its accounts audited by a chartered accountant under the Companies Act every year. A tax audit is needed in addition when turnover crosses the income tax limits.
Are small companies exempt from audit in India, as they are in the UK or Singapore?
No. India has no small-company audit exemption: every company, including a one person company, a small company and a foreign-owned subsidiary with little or no revenue, needs a statutory audit by a chartered accountant every year. What small companies and OPCs do get is lighter reporting - CARO 2020 does not apply to them, they need not prepare a cash flow statement, and auditor rotation applies only to listed and larger companies. Foreign parents used to the UK or Singapore exemption should budget for an Indian audit from the first financial year; book a call to plan the first audit and filing calendar.
When do CARO 2020, internal audit and Ind AS apply to a private company?
CARO 2020 applies to most companies, but not to small companies, OPCs, section 8 companies, or a private company (not a holding or subsidiary of a public company) whose paid-up capital and reserves are within Rs 1 crore, borrowings stay within Rs 1 crore throughout the year and total revenue is within Rs 10 crore. A private company needs an internal auditor under section 138 when the previous year's turnover was Rs 200 crore or more, or outstanding loans or borrowings reached Rs 100 crore or more at any time in that year. Ind AS is compulsory for listed companies and unlisted companies with net worth of Rs 250 crore or more, plus their holding, subsidiary, joint venture and associate companies in India; an Indian subsidiary does not move to Ind AS merely because its foreign parent reports under IFRS. Since 1 April 2023 every company's accounting software must also keep an audit trail that cannot be disabled. Book a call to check which of these apply to the company.
What is the audit applicability for an LLP?
An LLP needs a statutory audit when its turnover in the year exceeds Rs 40 lakh or its partners' contribution exceeds Rs 25 lakh, under rule 24(8) of the LLP Rules. Below both, the accounts can be signed by the designated partners without audit. A separate tax audit applies if turnover crosses the section 44AB limit; LLPs cannot use the 44AD presumptive scheme.
Is audit required for a proprietorship or partnership firm?
There is no statutory audit for a proprietorship or a partnership firm. A tax audit under section 44AB is needed when business turnover exceeds Rs 1 crore (Rs 10 crore where cash receipts and payments are each within 5%) or professional receipts exceed Rs 50 lakh, or where presumptive income is declared below the scheme rate and total income is above the basic exemption limit.
When does a trust need an audit?
A trust or institution registered for exemption must get its accounts audited when its income, before claiming the exemption, exceeds the basic exemption limit, and file the audit report in the prescribed form (Form 10B or 10BB under the 1961 rules) before the return. Missing it can cost the exemption for the year.
What is the penalty for not getting a tax audit done?
Section 271B allows a penalty of 0.5% of turnover or gross receipts, subject to a cap, unless there was reasonable cause. Getting the audit done and filed as soon as possible limits the damage.
Can a tax audit and a statutory audit be done by the same CA?
Yes, usually. A company's statutory auditor can also do its tax audit, subject to the professional rules on how many tax audits one CA may sign in a year.
