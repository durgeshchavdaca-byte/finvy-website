Leave Encashment Tax 2026: Is It Taxable, Rs 25 Lakh Exemption (Section 10(10AA), Now Section 19), New Regime and Calculation, when you encash decides whether you pay.

Leave encashment received while you are still in service is fully taxable as salary, but encashment at retirement or resignation is exempt for government employees and, for private-sector employees, exempt up to ₹25 lakh over a lifetime. The exemption is in section 10(10AA) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 and section 19 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, and it is available under both the old and new tax regimes. For non-government employees the exempt amount is the least of four limits, including ₹25 lakh and 10 months’ average salary.

/ Quick answers

What people search for.

  • Is leave encashment taxable? During service: yes, fully, as salary. At retirement or resignation: exempt for government employees; for others, exempt up to the least of four limits, with the balance taxable.
  • What is the leave encashment exemption limit? ₹25 lakh for non-government employees (from 1 April 2023, earlier ₹3 lakh), as a lifetime limit across all employers.
  • Is leave encashment exempt in the new tax regime? Yes. The retirement / resignation exemption is not among those withdrawn in the new regime.
  • How is the exempt amount calculated? Least of: the amount received; ₹25 lakh (less any exemption claimed earlier); 10 months’ average salary; and the cash equivalent of unavailed leave at up to 30 days for each completed year of service.
  • Is leave encashment paid to legal heirs taxable? No, encashment received by legal heirs after an employee’s death is not taxable in their hands.
  • Is TDS deducted? The employer deducts TDS on the taxable part along with salary; see TDS on salary 2026-27.

/ Planning points

Before you resign or retire.

  • Track the lifetime cap: keep records of exemption claimed at earlier exits; the ₹25 lakh is shared across all employers.
  • Spread arrears: where encashment includes arrears, relief under section 89 can reduce the tax.
  • Other exit payments: gratuity and PF have separate rules; see the gratuity calculator and PF withdrawal tax rules.
  • Regime choice: compare with the new vs old regime calculator.
  • Employers: apply the exemption correctly in payroll and report it in Form 130; see subsidiary payroll and benefits.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is leave encashment taxable?
Leave encashed during service is fully taxable as salary. At retirement or resignation it is fully exempt for government employees, and exempt for others up to the least of four limits, including Rs 25 lakh over a lifetime.
What is the leave encashment exemption limit for 2026?
Rs 25 lakh for non-government employees, a lifetime limit across all employers, in force since 1 April 2023 and unchanged since.
Is leave encashment exempt under the new tax regime?
Yes. The exemption for leave encashment at retirement or resignation is available under both the old and the new tax regime.
How do I calculate taxable leave encashment?
Take the least of the amount received, Rs 25 lakh (less any exemption claimed earlier), 10 months' average salary and the cash equivalent of unavailed leave at up to 30 days per completed year of service; that amount is exempt and the rest is taxable salary.
Is leave encashment received by legal heirs taxable?
No. Leave encashment paid to legal heirs after the employee's death is not taxable in their hands.
