/ GST-registered businesses · GST compliance · Updated 2026-11-25

GST Return Filing 2026: Due Dates (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, QRMP, CMP-08, GSTR-9), What Happens If a GST Return Is Not Filed, Late Fee Per Day, Maximum Cap and the 3-Year Filing Bar, nil returns are due too, and late fees run every day.

Most GST-registered businesses file two returns: GSTR-1 (sales) by the 11th and GSTR-3B (summary and tax payment) by the 20th of the next month, or quarterly under QRMP if turnover is up to ₹5 crore. Composition dealers file CMP-08 every quarter and GSTR-4 once a year, and most regular taxpayers above ₹2 crore turnover also file the annual GSTR-9 by 31 December. Returns are due even with no business in the period; late filing attracts a daily late fee and 18% interest on tax paid late.

/ How to file GST returns online

Step by step on the GST portal.

  • Log in at the GST portal with your GSTIN-linked user ID.
  • Reconcile purchases: accept or reject supplier invoices and check GSTR-2B; input tax credit is allowed only for invoices that appear in it; see input tax credit rules.
  • File GSTR-1 (or IFF) with invoice-wise B2B sales, B2C summaries, credit notes and HSN details.
  • Check GSTR-3B: it is auto-drafted from GSTR-1 and GSTR-2B; correct any genuine differences before filing.
  • Pay tax through the electronic cash and credit ledgers, then file with DSC or EVC.
  • Nil periods: file nil GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B even when there are no sales.

/ Late fees, interest and fixes

What missing a date costs.

  • Late fee: ₹50 a day (₹20 a day for nil returns) for GSTR-3B and GSTR-1, subject to caps linked to turnover.
  • Interest: 18% a year on tax paid after the due date.
  • Blocked actions: continued non-filing can block e-way bills and lead to cancellation of registration; see GST cancellation and revocation.
  • Notices: mismatches between GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B trigger ASMT-10 and DRC notices; see GST notice response.
  • Do you need GST at all? See GST registration thresholds.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

What is the due date for GST return filing?
GSTR-1 by the 11th and GSTR-3B by the 20th of the next month for monthly filers; under QRMP, GSTR-1 by the 13th and GSTR-3B by the 22nd or 24th (by state) of the month after the quarter.
Which GST returns does a regular business file?
GSTR-1 for sales and GSTR-3B for the summary and tax payment, monthly or quarterly, plus the annual GSTR-9 where turnover exceeds Rs 2 crore (optional below) and GSTR-9C self-certification above Rs 5 crore.
What is the GST e-invoice threshold, and is there a time limit to report e-invoices?
E-invoicing is mandatory for a business whose aggregate turnover exceeded INR 5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards (the limit fell to INR 5 crore from 1 August 2023); once crossed, it applies to all B2B invoices, export invoices and B2B credit and debit notes, even if turnover later falls. Each such document must be reported to an Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) to get an IRN and QR code - an invoice without a valid IRN is not a valid tax invoice and the buyer can lose input tax credit. Businesses with aggregate turnover of INR 10 crore or more must report each document within 30 days of its date (from 1 April 2025); after that the IRP rejects it. Government departments, banks and insurance companies, GTA, passenger transport, cinema admission and SEZ units are among the exempt categories. B2C invoices stay outside e-invoicing. How do I file a GST return online?
Log in to the GST portal, reconcile purchases with GSTR-2B, file GSTR-1 (or IFF), review the auto-drafted GSTR-3B, pay tax through the cash and credit ledgers and file with DSC or EVC.
Is a nil GST return mandatory?
Yes. A registered person must file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B even when there are no sales or purchases in the period; nil returns attract a lower late fee if filed late.
What happens if a GST return is not filed?
Late fee runs every day the return is late - Rs 50 a day (Rs 20 a day for a nil return) up to the caps per return - and 18% a year interest runs on any tax paid late. Beyond the money, the next period's return cannot be filed until the earlier one is, the GSTIN can be blocked from generating e-way bills, your buyers lose input tax credit because your invoices do not reach their GSTR-2B, and continued non-filing can lead to a show cause notice in REG-17 and cancellation of the registration. Filing the pending returns as early as possible stops the late fee growing and keeps revocation open if the registration has already been cancelled.
Can I file a GST return without paying the tax?
Not GSTR-3B: it can be filed only after the tax shown as payable is paid from the cash or credit ledger, so a return with tax due stays unfiled until payment. GSTR-1 (the sales statement) can be filed on its own without payment, and filing it on time keeps your buyers' input tax credit flowing. If cash is short, file GSTR-1, pay what you can and file GSTR-3B as soon as possible - interest runs only on the tax paid late. What is the late fee for GST returns?
Rs 50 a day (Rs 20 a day for nil returns) for GSTR-3B and GSTR-1, subject to caps linked to turnover, plus 18% a year interest on tax paid late.
What is the maximum late fee for GSTR-3B?
The daily late fee (Rs 50 a day, or Rs 20 a day for a nil return, split equally between CGST and SGST) stops at a cap per return based on the previous year's aggregate turnover: Rs 500 for a nil return, Rs 2,000 for turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore, Rs 5,000 for Rs 1.5 crore to Rs 5 crore, and Rs 10,000 above Rs 5 crore. Interest at 18% a year under section 50 runs separately on any tax paid late, and a return cannot be filed after three years from its due date (section 39(11)), so old pending returns should be cleared quickly.
Can I still file a GST return that is three years old?
No. Since the Finance Act, 2023 amendments (in force from 1 October 2023), GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-4, GSTR-5, GSTR-6, GSTR-7, GSTR-8 and GSTR-9 cannot be filed after three years from their own due date, and the GST portal now blocks them on a rolling basis. Counting from the due date: GSTR-3B for October 2023 (due 20 November 2023) is already closed; GSTR-1 for November 2023 closes after 11 December 2026 and GSTR-3B for November 2023 after 20 December 2026; the FY 2022-23 annual return (GSTR-9) closes after 31 December 2026. Quarterly (QRMP) filers should check the due date of each quarter. Once a return is barred, the tax and credit for that period can no longer be set right through the return - an officer can assess the period on a best-judgment basis or raise a demand, and continued non-filing can lead to cancellation of registration. So file every pending return that is still open now, pay any shortfall with interest (DRC-03 where it cannot go through the return), and keep the reconciliation ready in case a notice follows.
