UK Accountants Outsourcing to India: UK GDPR and IDTA Checklist, what the contract and transfer need.

UK accountancy practices can outsource bookkeeping and accounts work to India. Because India does not have a UK adequacy regulation, the practice needs an Article 28 processor contract, the International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or UK Addendum, a transfer risk assessment, and clear disclosure to clients.

/ The checklist

Before client data is accessed from India.

  • Processor contract meeting Article 28 of the UK GDPR.
  • IDTA, or the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, for the restricted transfer. New contracts after 21 September 2022 must use one of these.
  • Transfer risk assessment (the ICO publishes a tool).
  • Access controls: remote access to your systems, multi-factor authentication, no local downloads, access removed when staff change.
  • Update engagement letters and privacy notices to mention outsourced providers.
  • Record the arrangement in your data protection records.

Sources: ICO: international data transfer agreement; ACCA: data protection when extending your team; GOV.UK: Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is outsourcing to India GDPR compliant for UK accountants?
It can be, with an Article 28 processor contract, the IDTA or UK Addendum, a transfer risk assessment and appropriate security.
Does India have UK adequacy?
No, so a transfer mechanism such as the IDTA or UK Addendum is needed.
