Transfer pricing India, when parent is foreign.

Any Indian subsidiary of a foreign MNC with cross-border related-party transactions is in India's transfer pricing regime: Section 92 to 92F, Rules 10 to 10TE. Form 3CEB (Form 48 from tax year 2026-27) mandatory. Benchmarking study required. Transfer pricing scrutiny is common in India, so the benchmarking file needs to be ready before the report is signed. For groups above the threshold, Master File + CbCR + BEPS Pillar 2. Here's the practitioner's framework.

/ When Indian TP applies

Associated Enterprise + International Transaction.

India's TP applies when:

  • An Indian company has transactions with an Associated Enterprise (AE), AND
  • The transaction qualifies as an International Transaction or Specified Domestic Transaction.

Associated Enterprise (Section 92A):

  • Direct or indirect participation in management, control, or capital (26%+ shareholding, 26%+ voting, etc.).
  • Common management / control of both enterprises.
  • Loans of 51% or more of the borrower's total assets, or guarantees of 10% or more of its total borrowings.
  • Appointment of > 50% of board members.

Indian subsidiary of US parent: AE. Indian subsidiary of a UK parent held through a Singapore holdco: both the UK parent and the Singapore holdco are AEs.

International Transaction (Section 92B): any transaction between AEs that affects profits, income, losses, or assets — purchase/sale of goods, services, intangibles, lending/borrowing, guarantees, cost sharing, business restructuring.

Threshold for TP documentation: aggregate international transactions > INR 1 crore in the FY → Section 92D documentation required.

/ Form 3CEB (annual audit)

The core compliance.

Form 3CEB is a transfer pricing audit certificate issued by a Chartered Accountant, filed as part of the Indian company's annual tax return. Required for:

  • Every taxpayer that entered into any international transaction or specified domestic transaction in the year, whatever the amount (the INR 1 crore threshold only relaxes the detailed documentation, not the Form 3CEB report).
  • Due date: 31 October of the assessment year, one month before the 30 November return due date for transfer-pricing cases.

Form 3CEB covers:

  • List of all international transactions with each AE — by nature (goods, services, intangibles, financing, cost allocation, business restructuring).
  • Transaction value in INR + description.
  • Arm's-length method chosen (CUP, Resale Price Method, Cost Plus Method, TNMM, PSM, Other Method).
  • Benchmarking analysis supporting arm's-length conclusion.
  • Material facts and specific adjustments / documentation.

Underlying documentation: Section 92D requires detailed TP documentation to be maintained (not filed, but produced if asked): entity profile, industry analysis, functional analysis, economic analysis / benchmarking study. Must be contemporaneous (prepared by the time of filing Form 3CEB, not after a scrutiny notice).

/ Master File + CbCR (for large groups)

BEPS compliance.

Finance Act 2016 introduced BEPS-aligned Master File and Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) requirements.

Master File (Form 3CEAA): required for any Indian entity which is part of an international group where:

  • Consolidated group revenue in preceding year > INR 500 crore (~USD 60 million), AND
  • Aggregate value of international transactions > INR 50 crore OR international transactions pertaining to intangibles > INR 10 crore.

Contents: group overview, business strategy, group's intangibles, group's intercompany financing, group's financial + tax position.

CbCR (Form 3CEAD): required for any ultimate parent of an international group with consolidated revenue > INR 6,400 crore (~EUR 750 million, aligned with BEPS Action 13).

  • Primary filing obligation on the ultimate parent (if India).
  • Secondary filing (Form 3CEAC & 3CEAD) on Indian subsidiary of foreign parent if parent country has no CbCR regime or no information-sharing MoU.

Contents: country-by-country breakdown of revenue, profit, tax paid, employees, tangible assets.

BEPS Pillar 2 (Global Minimum Tax, 15%): applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million. India had not legislated GloBE rules (no QDMTT, IIR or UTPR) as of November 2026, so top-up tax on low-taxed Indian profits is collected by the parent's jurisdiction; plan for an Indian QDMTT if one is introduced. / APA (Advance Pricing Agreement)

Certainty for 5+5 years.

For taxpayers wanting certainty on TP position: Section 92CC Advance Pricing Agreement with CBDT.

  • Unilateral APA: with CBDT only.
  • Bilateral APA: between CBDT and competent authority of the other country (US, UK, Japan, Singapore — most useful).
  • Multilateral APA: three or more jurisdictions.

Duration: 5 years prospective + 4 years rollback = up to 9 years of certainty.

Benefits:

  • No TP scrutiny for covered transactions during APA period.
  • Removes double-taxation risk (bilateral APA).
  • Reduces litigation cost / management time.

Timeline: varies; bilateral APAs usually take longer because both tax authorities must agree. Most useful for groups with large recurring intercompany transactions.

/ Common TP mistakes foreign MNCs make in India

What triggers assessment.

  • Low operating margin on contract services: Indian subsidiary providing IT / BPO services to the foreign parent at a margin below the range shown by the comparables the officer selects, so the officer adds the difference to income.
  • Royalty rate on licensed brand / technology: too low (parent extracting value) or too high (shifting profit to parent). Benchmarking against royalty databases (e.g., RoyaltyStat, ktMINE) + comparability adjustments.
  • Guarantee fee for parent's bank guarantee: Indian subsidiary paying nil or symbolic guarantee fee to parent for parent-provided guarantee. Officers expect an arm's length fee, benchmarked to what an independent guarantor or bank would charge for a comparable guarantee.
  • Marketing intangibles (AMP / brand building): Indian subsidiary spending significantly on brand marketing in India but not getting marketing-service fee / IP reward from parent. AMP adjustments have been heavily litigated; courts rejected the 'bright line' test, but the issue still comes up in assessments.
  • Cost allocation without substance: parent allocating group HQ costs to Indian subsidiary without proper allocation key / benefit test. Transfer pricing officers typically challenge allocations that lack a benefit test.
  • Weak benchmarking: using too few comparables, including non-comparable sized companies, cherry-picking inter-quartile range.
  • Not filing Master File / CbCR when applicable: group revenue above threshold but local Indian team not aware of global filing status.

FAQ

Common questions, answered.

Is Form 3CEB mandatory for every foreign-subsidiary in India?
Effectively yes - any Indian subsidiary of a foreign parent that has international transactions (parent charges management fee, royalty, interest, service fee, or buys/sells goods to Indian subsidiary) needs the Form 3CEB report for any international transaction, whatever the amount; the INR 1 crore threshold only decides whether detailed documentation is required. It is filed alongside Form 3CD and ITR-6. Due 31 October of the assessment year (31 October 2026 for FY 2025-26).
What is the transfer pricing documentation threshold in India?
Detailed transfer pricing documentation (functional analysis, benchmarking, agreements) must be kept where the aggregate value of international transactions with associated enterprises exceeds INR 1 crore in the year. Below that, documentation is lighter, but the accountant's report (Form 48, formerly Form 3CEB) is still filed for any international transaction, and prices must still be at arm's length.
What are the transfer pricing documentation requirements in India?
Records of the ownership structure, the group's business, the nature and terms of each related-party transaction, a functional, asset and risk analysis, the method selected and the comparables used, the agreements and invoices, and any assumptions or adjustments. From tax year 2026-27 these sit under the Income-tax Rules, 2026; the documentation must exist by the due date of the accountant's report.
When is the master file required in India?
When the group's consolidated revenue exceeds INR 500 crore and either its international transactions exceed INR 50 crore, or transactions in intangibles exceed INR 10 crore. The master file and the country-by-country report are separate from the local documentation and the accountant's report.
What is Master File and when do I need it?
Form 3CEAA - detailed documentation of the entire international group (business, intangibles, intercompany financing, tax position). Required if (a) group consolidated revenue > INR 500 crore in preceding year AND (b) aggregate international transactions > INR 50 crore OR intangible-related > INR 10 crore. Filed by Indian subsidiary; prepared based on group data from parent.
What is CbCR and when does India require it?
Country-by-Country Report (Form 3CEAD) under BEPS Action 13 - country-wise breakdown of revenue, profit, tax paid, headcount, tangible assets for the whole international group. Required for groups with consolidated revenue > INR 6,400 crore (~EUR 750M). Primary filing by ultimate parent; secondary filing by Indian subsidiary if parent country has no CbCR regime.
Is APA worth the cost for a foreign MNC in India?
For groups with large recurring intercompany transactions (e.g., annual royalty > USD 5M, or IT services engagement with 1000+ Indian headcount), yes - 5+4 years of certainty + removal of double-taxation risk + reduced litigation outweigh the 24-36 month filing process + professional fees. For smaller / variable transactions, standard annual Form 3CEB may suffice.
How much is a typical TP adjustment in India?
There is no typical figure: an adjustment depends on the transaction, the comparables the officer selects and the margin or price your documentation supports. Contract service providers whose margin falls below the comparable range, royalty and management-fee payments without a benefit test and benchmarking, and unpriced guarantees or loans are the areas most often adjusted. A contemporaneous TP study, the safe harbour rules where they fit, or an advance pricing agreement are the ways to get certainty.
What is the penalty for not filing Form 3CEB or keeping transfer pricing documentation?
Under the 1961 Act provisions carried into the 2025 Act: failing to furnish the accountant's report (Form 3CEB, now Form 48) attracts a penalty of Rs 1 lakh; failing to keep or maintain the prescribed documentation, or reporting a transaction incorrectly, can cost 2% of the value of each international transaction; and failing to produce the documents when the officer asks can cost a further 2%. Any adjustment to the price is taxed on top, with interest, and can attract under-reporting penalties.
